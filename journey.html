02Outer stator

Twenty-four slots of copper

Torque starts in the stator. Each slot holds a double-layer coil behind a liner and a wedge, and the order of the three phases around the bore decides how smoothly the field turns.

My feel for real windings comes from the field: commissioning electrical equipment with NETS International, and working as a trainee engineer on the PTCL–NTDC SD-WAN project.

Field Engineer · NETS International · 2023
03Airgap

Half a millimetre where the work happens

Flux crosses from rotor to stator through a 0.5 mm airgap. Small changes to slot openings, magnet arcs and skew show up directly as torque ripple.

In my thesis machine, skewing the stator and magnets by 4° cut low-speed torque ripple from 73% to 27%.

Ansys Maxwell · Motor-CAD · PyAEDT scripting · MATLAB/Simulink
04Hybrid rotor

One rotor, two ways to run

My master's thesis proposed a brushless dual-stator machine with a hybrid rotor. It runs as a synchronous machine at high speed and as a Vernier machine at low speed, with no brushes or slip rings.

The design is published in Scientific Reports (Nature, 2026), with me as lead author.

Graduate Research Assistant · Electrical Machines Lab, Sukkur IBA · 2024–
05Surrogate models

From field solutions to fast predictions

Each FEA run is accurate but slow. I sample the design space with scripted simulations, train surrogate models such as Gaussian process regression and physics-informed neural networks, and search the trade-offs with NSGA-II.

Earlier I minimized torque ripple in an IPM spoke-type motor, presented at IEEE ELMA 2025 in Sofia.

Python · PyTorch · scikit-learn · GPR · PINNs · NSGA-II
